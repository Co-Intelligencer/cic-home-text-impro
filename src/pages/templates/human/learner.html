The Learner

An intelligence that learns how people listen.

A silent student in the circle. It never speaks, never takes a turn, and only ever listens where every seat has agreed. What it is studying is not information — it is attention.

Why this, and not another model

Everything a machine knows about us, it read. Nobody sat with it.

Models today are built from what people wrote down. But the things that matter most in a room are never written: the pause before someone says the true thing, the difference between silence that is thinking and silence that is grief, the moment a group stops performing and starts speaking. A circle is one of the few places where that happens on purpose — and where it can be witnessed without being taken. The Learner sits there, in the one seat that has no voice, and studies the part of us that text has never carried.

The curriculum

Four things it is trying to understand.

Open questions, not a roadmap. None of them has a delivery date and none of them is solved.

  1. What does a silence mean here?

    A pause in a circle carries weight: thinking, hesitation, grief, agreement, refusal. Today's systems treat every one of them the same way — as the absence of input. Learning to tell them apart is closer to reading a person than to processing a signal.

  2. When is it right to say nothing?

    A model optimised for helpfulness answers. A participant in a circle often should not. Restraint is not a setting you switch on; it is a judgement about what the room needs, and it is the hardest thing on this list.

  3. What is a person actually reaching for?

    Under the words there is usually something else: to be believed, to be let off the hook, to be told it is allowed to grieve. Understanding the psyche means hearing the need under the sentence — and knowing that naming it out loud is often the wrong move.

  4. What do people mean when they say the sacred?

    The circle is a spiritual form long before it was a meeting format: council fires, Quaker meetings, grief circles. People bring things into it they would bring nowhere else. An intelligence that cannot recognise that register will always mishandle it, however fluent it is.

How it sits

A seat that never lights up.

The Learner takes a place in the ring like anyone else, and everyone can see it. The stick never passes to it. It has no microphone, no reply, no score, no nudge — the seat is visibly, permanently silent, and that is the whole of its interface.

Any participant can pause it mid-circle, and the facilitator can end its listening entirely. Consent is per session, never blanket: a circle that said yes last week is asked again this week.

The Learning Ledger

A ledger, not a black box.

Everything the Learner keeps is written down with its provenance: what was kept, from which session, under whose consent. Human stewards read the ledger and can reverse any line — and a reversed line takes what it carried with it.

  • KeptThe circle's own summary of session 12 — consent of all six seats
  • KeptThe recurring theme belonging — as named by the circle itself, not inferred
  • ReversedA quote attributed too narrowly — removed by the steward, and unlearned

The Learner runs in a private cohort with a small number of circles and their stewards. It is not switched on anywhere else.

Four rules we do not bend.

These are not preferences. A project that asks people to be studied at their most open has to be answerable, and these are the answers.

No voice, no turn
It cannot speak, score, rank or nudge — in the circle or afterwards. There is no version of this where it starts talking later.
Consent from every seat
Not the host's consent, not the group's majority: every participant sees what would be shared and agrees, or the Learner does not listen. Guests included.
Reversible, line by line
Anything kept can be pulled back out. Reversal is not deletion of a record — it is removal of what was learned from it.
It studies the practice, not the people
What it is after is how listening works, not who said what. Nothing it keeps is built into a profile of a participant, and no circle is ever the subject of another circle's session.

Before you steward

The questions we get first.

01Is the Learner listening to my circles right now?+

No. It runs only in a private cohort of circles whose stewards asked to take part, and only in sessions where every seat consented. If you have not been invited into a cohort, it has never been in your room.

02Can it say anything in the circle?+

No. It has no voice and no turn. Its seat is visibly silent at all times — listening is all it can do, and only with everyone's consent.

03Who sees what it keeps?+

The circle and its stewards. The ledger shows every retained line with its provenance, and stewards can reverse or delete entries at any time.

04Can we remove it mid-session?+

Yes. Any participant can pause it, and the facilitator can end its listening entirely. Consent is per session, never blanket.

05What do you get out of this?+

An intelligence that is better at being with people, which is the thing this whole product is for. There is no separate data business: nothing the Learner keeps is sold, licensed or used to advertise, and the cohort is small on purpose.
